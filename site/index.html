Your desktop, under the real sky.

A conky desktop for GNOME. The wallpaper is redrawn through the day for the real sun, moon and weather where you are; frosted cards show the time, weather and your machine; and a little glob pet lives on them, reacting to the time, the weather, your music and your typing.

Install View on GitHub

Free and open source (MIT) · GNOME on X11 · about 2 % of one CPU core

Two looks, day and night

The card palette follows the sky through the Catppuccin flavours: Mocha at night, Latte by day, in the accent colours you pick.

Glass: frosted liquid-glass panes over the wallpaper. Cards: solid Catppuccin cards.

Four painted scenes

Each scene is a painting, traced once into layers and relit live for the hour, the season and the weather. Snow settles only when it really snows (or in a high-latitude winter).

Hour by hour (24 renders)

Meet the globs

Pick one, a different one every day (glob = random), or the one that matches your scene (glob = scene). They sleep, dance to music, watch videos, type along with you, wear sunglasses in strong sun and masks when the air is bad, and get grumpy if you wake or ignore them. The same pet can sit in your top panel too.

The cards

Six on by default, four more you can switch on; they wrap into a second column when the screen is full. Hardware is detected automatically.

All ten cards at once, and the colour sets

Making your own card is one small Lua file: lua/cards/README.md. Your own pet: lua/glob/README.md.

Install

git clone https://github.com/SingletLinkage/living-sky ~/living-sky
cd ~/living-sky
./install.sh

The installer checks what living-sky needs and offers to install what's missing (packages through apt, dnf, pacman or zypper after asking; the conky AppImage if your distro's conky is too old; the fonts), asks a few questions, adds the Living Sky settings app and a login entry, and starts it. --yes takes the defaults, --dry-run shows what it would do.

Requirements

  • GNOME on X11 (Wayland isn't supported yet). The panel pet works on GNOME 42–48.
  • conky 1.19+ with Lua cairo (or the conky AppImage, which the installer can fetch).
  • Python 3 with numpy, OpenCV, Pillow, PyGObject and python-xlib; GTK 4 + libadwaita for the settings app.
  • curl, xrandr, gsettings; fonts JetBrains Mono, FiraCode Nerd Font and Bebas Neue.

Uninstall

./uninstall.sh                # keeps your settings
./uninstall.sh --purge        # removes them too
./uninstall.sh --purge-deps   # also the fonts and conky the installer downloaded

Use and configure

Open Living Sky from your apps (or right-click the panel pet): pages for the look, colours and pet, cards, location, and the system side. It edits one plain file, ~/.config/living-sky/living-sky.conf, keeping its comments, so editing it by hand works too; the running desktop picks up changes within about 5 seconds.

living-sky start | stop | restart | status
living-sky settings              # the settings window
living-sky set scene camp        # change one setting (set --list: all of them)
living-sky get scene
living-sky config                # the file in your editor
living-sky logs [-f]             # recent log lines and errors
living-sky doctor                # what was detected (for bug reports)
living-sky preview --demo        # a PNG of your desktop with personal details hidden

Look

theme glass | cards · scene lake | forest | city | camp · pixel on | off · season auto or one of four · colors auto, lavender, sakura, mint, peach, ocean, or two hex colours · units · clock 24h | 12h

Cards

card_<id> = on | off for clock, weather, calendar, media, cpu, memory, disks, gpu, battery, network · cards = network, clock, … sets the order

Pet

glob mimi | pip | bun | nyx | cubo | ember | random | scene | off

Sky and location

lat, lon, name (or looked up once from your IP) · interval_min minutes between renders

Privacy

  • Location: one IP lookup (ipinfo.io, falling back to ipapi.co) unless you set coordinates; then cached.
  • Weather and air quality: Open-Meteo, no account, every 15 minutes.
  • The pet notices only which kind of window is focused (terminal, editor, files, browser), never titles, and only that a key went down, never which. Nothing else leaves your machine.
